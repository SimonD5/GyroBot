The GyroBot project integrates a real eco-design approach to limit our carbon footprint. Instead of making parts far away, all the structure is manufactured locally by 3D printing using PLA, a bio-based and recyclable plastic. To make the sorting easier at the end of life (FS6), recycling symbols are modeled directly in relief on the CAD parts. To go further in accessibility, each material or sorting instruction has its own geometric logo in relief and a color code. These colors were specifically chosen to be easily seen by colorblind people (yellow, dark blue, white, black, magenta). For the electronic part, designing and routing a custom motherboard allows us to connect the microcontrollers and components directly through copper tracks. This avoids using dozens of flying wires. It reduces the amount of plastic cables used and makes the system much tougher.
The robot was also designed to last in time and to fight against obsolescence (meaning avoiding throwing it away at the first broken component). All the architecture allows us to easily access fragile parts to change them quickly (FP6). The assembly is done using only standard screws, so the structure can be disassembled very simply (FC7) with basic screwdrivers without special tools (FS5). The battery, for example, can be removed very easily to be sent to its specific recycling channel which makes recycling much easier (FC8). This design choice allows us to respect the European WEEE directive (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) which imposes strict rules to limit the pollution of this kind of system (FC9).
Finally, beyond the environmental impact, the GyroBot has a real social utility focused on safety at work. The main goal is to map inaccessible, unstable or dangerous places instead of humans. Whether it is to explore a building for a rescue or to inspect unreachable industrial environments, all the necessary spatial data is collected by the robot without ever putting the lives of rescue teams or technicians in danger.
